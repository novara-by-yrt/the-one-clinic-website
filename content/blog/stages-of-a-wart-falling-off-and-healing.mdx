<span style="font-weight: 400;">The stages of a wart falling off happen in clear steps: colour change, drying and shrinking, blister or scab formation, detachment, and final healing. Watching these stages closely helps you understand how your skin is recovering and shows that your body is fighting the virus naturally.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">The HPV virus causes warts, and they may appear on the hands, feet, or other areas. They don’t vanish instantly, and knowing what to expect at each stage can make the healing process less worrying. </span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">In this blog, we’ll guide you through each stage of a wart falling off so that you can care for your skin safely and effectively.</span>
<h2><b>What Causes a Wart to Fall Off?</b></h2>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">A wart falls off when the virus inside it dies. This happens slowly as the body works to remove the infected skin.</span>
<ul>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Your immune system plays a big role. It learns to recognise the virus and begins to attack it. When this happens, the wart stops growing.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Treatment helps speed up this process. Freezing, acid treatments, or medical care damage the wart so the body can more easily remove it.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">The blood supply to the wart also stops. Without blood, the wart dries out and begins to shrink.</span></li>
</ul>

<h2><b>What are the 5 Stages of a Wart Falling Off?</b></h2>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Understanding how a wart disappears can make the healing process less worrying. Here are the 5 stages of a wart falling off and what to expect at each step</span>
<h3><b>1. Stage 1: Colour Change and Early Signs</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">The wart starts to change colour. It may look white, grey, brown, or even black. Small black dots can appear inside the wart. These are tiny blood vessels that are no longer working.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">The wart may look darker than the skin around it. This means the blood supply is stopping. The surface can feel different. It may seem drier and less raised than before.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">This stage is normal. It shows the wart is beginning to die, or that the treatment or the body is working. Do not touch or pick it. Let the process happen on its own.</span>
<h3><b>2. Stage 2: Drying and Shrinking</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">The wart starts to dry out. It no longer feels soft or spongy. It slowly gets smaller each day. This is a good sign of healing.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">The surface can become rough. Small bits of skin may peel or flake off. The wart may look flatter than before. Edges are less clear.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">It can feel hard when you touch it. This means the damaged skin is dying. Do not cut, pull, or scratch the wart, and keep the area clean. Picking can cause pain or spread the virus.</span>
<h3><b>3. Stage 3: Blister or Scab (Common After Freezing)</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">If a doctor freezes the wart, a blister may form under or around it. This can happen within one day. The blister may look clear or dark red; both are normal and show the skin is reacting.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">As days pass, the blister dries out and turns into a scab. This helps lift the wart away from healthy skin underneath.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Do not pop the blister or pull the scab. Let it dry and heal on its own to avoid marks or infection.</span>
<h3><b>4. Stage 4: Wart Falls Off</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Once the wart tissue is fully dead, it will fall off by itself. Sometimes it drops off, or sometimes the scab comes away gently.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">You may see a small dip or soft spot in the skin. This is common, especially with deeper warts.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">The skin underneath may look pink or red, showing that new skin is forming.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Keep the area clean and dry, and do not scratch or pick at the new skin.</span>
<h3><b>5. Stage 5: Final Healing</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">After the wart has fallen off, the skin continues to heal. This stage takes a little time and patience.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">The colour slowly fades back to normal. The skin surface becomes smooth again.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Normal skin lines, like fingerprints or foot lines, start to return, showing that the skin is healthy.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Healing can take days or a few weeks. Once healed, the area should feel and look like normal skin again.</span>
<h2><b>How Do You Know When a Wart Is Falling Off? </b></h2>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">The wart starts to get smaller than before. This shows it is no longer growing, and the body is fighting it. The signs may include:</span>
<ul>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">The colour begins to change. It may turn white, brown, or black. </span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">The surface feels dry and rough rather than soft or fleshy.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Skin on top may peel or flake away.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">The wart may feel harder to touch. </span></li>
</ul>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">These are good signs. They show your body is winning and the wart is on its way out.</span>
<h2><b>What Colour Is a Wart When It’s Dying?</b></h2>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">When a wart is falling off, it changes its colour. Here are some colours that indicate the wart is healing:</span>
<ul>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>White or grey at first</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: The top layers of skin die and dry out, often after freezing or acid treatments.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Brown or black spots</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Tiny clotted blood vessels that fed the wart are dying; this is normal.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Pink or red underneath</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Healthy skin begins to show as the wart heals.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Return to normal skin colour</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Over time, the area blends back with the surrounding skin.</span></li>
</ul>
<b>Tip</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Do not pick or scratch the wart. Let it peel naturally to avoid scarring and spreading the virus.</span>
<h2><b>How Long Does a Wart Take to Fall Off?</b></h2>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">The time it takes for a wart to fall off can vary depending on the treatment, location, and size of the wart.</span>
<ul>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Freezing (Cryotherapy)</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Usually 7 to 14 days for the wart or scab to fall off.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Acid Treatments (Salicylic Acid)</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Can take 4 to 12 weeks of daily application for complete removal.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Foot Warts (Plantar Warts)</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Often take longer because they are thicker and grow inward.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Size and Location Matter</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Larger warts or warts in tricky spots may need more time or multiple treatments.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Consistency Helps</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Following the treatment instructions daily speeds up healing.</span></li>
</ul>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Be patient and avoid picking at the wart, even if it looks almost gone.</span>
<h2><b>What Factors Can Slow The Healing of Warts?</b></h2>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Some things can make a wart take longer to heal, so it’s good to know what might slow the process:</span>
<ul>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Picking or cutting the wart</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Touching or trying to remove it can irritate the skin, spread the virus, and delay healing.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Stopping treatment too early</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Whether using salicylic acid or other home remedies, not completing the full course can prevent the wart from falling off completely.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Weak immune system</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Your body needs a strong immune response to fight the virus. Illness, stress, or certain medications can slow this.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Thick or deep warts</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Plantar warts on the soles of your feet or older, bigger warts may take longer to respond to treatment.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Location of the wart</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Warts on pressure points, like hands or feet, may face constant friction, which can slow the healing process.</span></li>
</ul>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">By being patient and following the treatment properly, most warts will eventually fall off, and the skin can fully heal.</span>

 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Spreads or grows.</span></li>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Apply 17% salicylic acid daily after soaking the wart in warm water. The wart will slowly shrink, peel, and disappear over several weeks.</span>

<ul>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="2"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Cryotherapy by a professional</span></li>
