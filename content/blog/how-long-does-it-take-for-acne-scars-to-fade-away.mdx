Acne scars do not all fade at the same rate, so the timeline varies for everyone. Flat marks left after a spot, such as red or brown patches, often fade on their own within 3 to 24 months. Raised or pitted scars that change the skin's texture usually do not fade fully without treatment. So, how long does it take for acne scars to fade? The real answer depends on the type of scar, your skin tone, and how you care for your skin. Knowing the difference between a temporary mark and a true scar is the first step to setting realistic expectations. This guide explains the typical timelines, what slows healing down, and how to fade acne scars faster.
<h2>Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
 	<li>Flat marks (red, pink, or brown) often fade on their own in 3 to 24 months.</li>
 	<li>True scars that change the skin's texture usually need treatment to improve.</li>
 	<li>Around one-third of acne scars fade within six months without any treatment.</li>
 	<li>Skin tone, age, sun exposure, and picking all affect how long scars last.</li>
 	<li>Professional treatments like peels, microneedling, and laser can speed up fading.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Do acne scars fade over time?</h3>
Flat marks usually do. In fact, around one-third of acne scars improve within six months without any treatment. After about two years, though, the scars that are left are unlikely to change much on their own. True textured scars need a little help to improve.
<h3>Acne scar healing time with treatment</h3>
When using professional treatments, many individuals see improved skin texture and brightness within 4 to 8 weeks, with further improvements within 3 to 6 months as collagen production increases. Professional treatments, such as microneedling, are usually done in several sessions, spaced 4 weeks apart.

<h2>Acne Marks vs Acne Scars: What Is the Difference?</h2>
<h3>Post-acne marks (colour changes)</h3>
These flat marks are the most common type and the quickest to fade.
<ul>
 	<li>Brown or dark marks (often called post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation) can fade in about 3 to 24 months.</li>
 	<li>Red or pink marks (often called post-inflammatory erythema) usually settle over a few months to a year.</li>
</ul>
So how long do acne marks take to fade? Most clear within a few months to two years, and darker skin tones often take a little longer.
<h3>True acne scars (texture changes)</h3>
True scars change the skin's surface and rarely fade fully on their own. They fall into two groups:

Atrophic (sunken) scars are the most common. These include:
<ul>
 	<li><strong>Ice pick scars:</strong> small, deep, narrow pits.</li>
 	<li><strong>Boxcar scars:</strong> wider dents with clear edges.</li>
 	<li><strong>Rolling scars:</strong> shallow, wave-like dips in the skin.</li>
</ul>
<strong>Hypertrophic and keloid (raised) scars</strong> are firm, raised areas caused by too much collagen. These are more common on the chest and back.
<h2>What Affects How Long Acne Scars Last?</h2>
No two people heal at the same speed. So, how long do acne scars last? It depends on several things:
<ul>
 	<li><strong>Scar type:</strong> marks fade faster than textured scars.</li>
 	<li><strong>Acne severity:</strong> deeper, more inflamed spots leave longer-lasting scars.</li>
 	<li><strong>Skin tone:</strong> darker tones are more prone to lingering dark marks.</li>
 	<li><strong>Age:</strong> younger skin produces collagen more quickly, so it tends to heal more quickly.</li>
 	<li><strong>Sun exposure:</strong> UV light makes dark marks worse and slows fading.</li>
 	<li><strong>Aftercare:</strong> gentle skincare and daily sun protection speed things up; picking slows them down.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Do Acne Scars Go Away on Their Own?</h2>
This depends on the type. Do acne scars go away without any help? Flat marks often do, given enough time. But true scars that change the skin's texture usually stay unless treated.

Do acne scars go away naturally? Colour-based marks can fade naturally over months, helped by sun protection and a gentle routine. Sunken or raised scars, on the other hand, rarely smooth out on their own, because the skin's structure has changed. It is also worth being realistic: even with treatment, scars can be made far less visible, but they may not disappear completely.

<h2>How to Fade Acne Scars?</h2>
The right plan depends on whether you are treating marks or true scars. Learning how to fade acne scars usually means pairing good skincare with professional treatment for deeper scars.
<h3>Skincare that helps marks fade</h3>
To deal with flat marks, one can try the following approach:
<ul>
 	<li>Apply sunscreen regularly to prevent skin spots from darkening.</li>
 	<li>Use gentle chemical exfoliants like AHAs and salicylic acid.</li>
 	<li>Apply brightening agents such as vitamin C, niacinamide, and azelaic acid.</li>
 	<li>Do not pick or scrub because it makes the spots worse.</li>
 	<li>Be patient, as marks fade slowly rather than overnight.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Professional treatments for deeper scars</h3>
Textured scars usually need clinical treatment to improve. Doctor-led options often include:
<ul>
 	<li><strong><a href="/treatments/chemical-peels-leicester">Chemical peels</a>:</strong> a solution removes dead skin cells and helps fade marks and soften shallow scars.</li>
 	<li><strong>Microneedling and RF microneedling (Morpheus8):</strong> tiny needles create controlled micro-injuries that stimulate collagen production, while radiofrequency energy targets the deeper layers to smooth scars and firm the skin.</li>
 	<li><strong>Laser and IPL resurfacing:</strong> targeted light energy resurfaces the skin, breaks down pigment, and stimulates new collagen.</li>
 	<li><strong>Fractional skin resurfacing:</strong> controlled energy remodels scar tissue and helps rebuild smoother skin over a course of sessions.</li>
</ul>
These treatment options primarily work by encouraging the skin to heal and produce new collagen. It takes a course of treatment, and an initial skin analysis will determine the right form of treatment for your specific scar tissue and skin tone. Certain medical clinics even provide skin boosters.
<h2>What to Expect and Possible Risks</h2>
Acne scar treatments are generally safe when carried out by trained professionals, but it helps to know what to expect:
<ul>
 	<li>Most treatments need more than one session to see clear results.</li>
 	<li>Mild redness, swelling, or sensitivity is common for a few days afterwards.</li>
 	<li>People with deeper skin tones have a slightly higher risk of temporary colour changes, so an experienced practitioner and a patch test are important.</li>
 	<li>Results build gradually over weeks and months, not instantly.</li>
 	<li>Treatment can improve scars but may not remove them completely.</li>
 	<li>Active breakouts may need to settle first, as new spots can cause new scars.</li>
</ul>
For deeper or stubborn scars, it is wise to see a doctor or skin specialist who can confirm the type of your scar and recommend a safe treatment plan.
<h2>Tips to Help Acne Scars Fade Faster</h2>
A few simple habits make a real difference:
<ul>
 	<li>Treat active acne early to stop new scars forming.</li>
 	<li>Never pick or squeeze spots.</li>
 	<li>Wear sunscreen every day, even in winter.</li>
 	<li>Keep your routine gentle and consistent.</li>
 	<li>Ask a professional before starting strong treatments.</li>
 	<li>Give it time, as most scars fade gradually.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Conclusion</h2>
So, how long does it take for acne scars to fade? Flat marks often fade on their own within 3 to 24 months, while sunken or raised scars usually need professional treatment to improve. The exact timeline depends on your scar type, skin tone, age, and aftercare. With patience, good skincare, and the right treatment, most acne scars can be made far less noticeable over time.
