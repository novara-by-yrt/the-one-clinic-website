<span style="font-weight: 400;">You can safely get rid of warts with professional treatments, including freezing (cryotherapy), laser removal, and medicated creams, as well as effective home remedies. </span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Warts are harmless skin growths that are caused by the HPV virus, and while many go away on their own, treatment can help speed up the process, reduce discomfort, and prevent spreading. </span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">In this blog, you’ll learn what warts are, why they appear, the best ways to remove them, and how to stop them from coming back.</span>
<h2><b>What Are Warts?</b></h2>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Warts are small lumps that grow on the skin. They are usually harmless but can look rough or bumpy.</span>
<h3><b>Different Types of Warts</b></h3>
<ul>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Common warts</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Small, round, rough bumps on hands, knees, or elbows.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Flat warts</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Smooth, flat, and often yellowish, found on the face or hands.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Plantar warts</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Grow on the feet, often painful because your body weight pushes them in.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Filiform warts</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Thin, long warts that appear on the face.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Mosaic warts</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Groups of small warts clustered together, usually on hands or feet.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Subungual or periungual warts</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Found under or around nails.</span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Mucosal wart</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">s: Found on the lips, inside the cheeks, or sometimes the genital areas.</span></li>
</ul>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Warts can appear on anyone at any age, but children and teenagers get them more often. They may fade on their own over time, but some can last months or even years without treatment.</span>
<h2><b>What are the Main Causes of Warts?</b></h2>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">There are various factors that may cause wart formation. They include:</span>
<h3><b>1. Human Papillomavirus (HPV)</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">The HPV virus, which infects the top layer of your skin, may cause warts. The virus makes skin cells grow faster, forming a small bump. Different HPV types cause different kinds of warts.</span>
<h3><b>2. Direct Skin Contact</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">You can catch warts by touching someone who already has them. Even a quick handshake or skin-to-skin contact can spread the virus to healthy skin.</span>
<h3><b>3. Indirect Contact with Surfaces</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">HPV can survive on surfaces like gym floors, swimming pool areas, or towels. Touching these contaminated surfaces may give you warts, especially if you have a small cut or scratch.</span>
<h3><b>4. Weak Immune System</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">People with weaker immunity, like those with diabetes or on certain medications, are more likely to get warts. Their immune system struggles to fight off the virus, so warts appear more easily.</span>
<h3><b>5. Lifestyle Factors</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">If you pick or scratch at the existing warts, it may make them worse or spread them to other areas. Moist or damp environments, like sweaty socks or gloves, can also encourage wart growth.</span>
<h2><b>How to Get Rid of Warts? Professional Treatments</b></h2>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">If you want to remove warts safely, here are the treatment options that the experts may choose:</span>
<h3><b>1. Cryotherapy (Freezing)</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">A doctor applies liquid nitrogen to the wart, which freezes it. This causes the skin to form a blister, lifting the wart off. Most people need a few sessions, spaced weeks apart, for stubborn warts. It’s quick, usually painless, and safe for most areas except very sensitive skin.</span>
<h3><b>2. Laser Treatment</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">A focused laser beam destroys the wart tissue and the tiny blood vessels that feed it. Laser is a very precise, effective method and is often used when warts do not respond to other treatments. Healing is gradual, and skin may be slightly red for a few days.</span>
<h3><b>3. Chemical Treatments</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Doctors use strong acids, like trichloroacetic acid, applied carefully to the wart. The acid peels away layers of the wart slowly without harming the surrounding skin. This process usually takes several appointments for full removal.</span>
<h3><b>4. Curettage and Electrosurgery</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">The doctor scrapes the wart away using a small surgical tool, sometimes using an electric current to burn the tissue. This works quickly for stubborn warts, but a local anaesthetic is used to make the procedure comfortable. Slight redness and healing marks are normal afterwards.</span>
<h3><b>5. Immunotherapy</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">For warts that keep coming back, doctors can inject medicines or apply creams that help the body’s immune system fight the virus. Over time, this can reduce wart size and prevent new warts from forming. It can take a few weeks to see full results.</span>
<h2><b>How To Remove Warts? Home Remedies and OTC Treatments</b></h2>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Home treatments can help remove warts, but they need time and daily care. Results are slow, and these options may not work for stubborn or long-lasting warts.</span>
<h3><b>1. Salicylic Acid (Wart Paints, Gels, or Plasters)</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Salicylic acid is the most common pharmacy treatment in the UK. It works by slowly peeling away the wart layer by layer. You apply it daily for several weeks. Always protect the healthy skin around the wart.</span>
<h3><b>2. Duct Tape Method</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Covering a wart with strong tape can help soften it over time. After a few days, the skin is soaked and gently rubbed. This method needs patience and can take many weeks to show results.</span>
<h3><b>3. At-Home Freezing Kits</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">These kits freeze the wart using cold gas. They are weaker than doctor treatments but may help small warts. Some warts need more than one application to fully go away.</span>
<h3><b>4. Warm Water Soaking and Filing</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Soaking the wart in warm water softens the skin. Gently filing dead skin can help treatments work better. Always use a clean file and never share it with others.</span>
<h3><b>Important Safety Tips</b></h3>
<ul>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Never cut, burn, or pick a wart yourself. Do not use strong treatments on the face or private areas. </span></li>
 	<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">If you notice that the pain is increasing and the wart is not improving at all, professional care is safer.</span></li>
</ul>
<h2><b>How to Prevent Warts?</b></h2>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Warts spread easily, but simple daily habits can help protect your skin and others around you.</span>
<h3><b>1. Avoid Touching or Picking Warts</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Picking or scratching a wart can spread the virus to nearby skin. It may also cause pain, infection, or scarring. It is best to leave warts alone.</span>
<h3><b>2. Wash Hands Regularly</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Always wash your hands after touching a wart or applying any treatment. This helps stop the virus from spreading to other parts of your body.</span>
<h3><b>3. Do Not Share Personal Items</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Do not share towels, socks, shoes, nail tools, or pumice stones. The virus can live on these items and pass from one person to another.</span>
<h3><b>4. Cover Warts in Public Areas</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">If you have a wart on your foot, keep it covered when swimming, in gyms, and in shared showers. Wearing flip-flops can reduce the risk of spread.</span>
<h3><b>5. Keep Skin Healthy and Protected</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Cuts and cracks in the skin make it easier for the virus to enter. Keep your skin clean, dry, and moisturised to lower the risk.</span>
<h3><b>6. HPV Vaccination</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Getting the HPV vaccine may protect against certain types of the virus that cause warts. It is recommended for eligible age groups and helps reduce future risk.</span>
<h2><b>With the Right Care, You Can Get Rid Of Warts. </b></h2>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Wart formation is common and usually harmless, but their presence can be bothersome or uncomfortable. Professional solutions such as cryotherapy, laser removal, chemical treatments, and immunotherapy can help get rid of warts safely.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Safe and effective treatments include at-home options like salicylic acid, duct tape, and freezing kits, as well as for stubborn, painful, or hard-to-reach warts, consulting a professional is the best way to get results.</span>

<h2><b>FAQs</b></h2>
<h3><b>1. What kills warts fast?</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">The fastest way to remove warts is medical treatment. A doctor can freeze the wart with liquid nitrogen or remove it using a laser or minor surgery. These methods work much quicker than home treatments.</span>
<h3><b>2. Do warts go away on their own?</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Yes, many warts go away on their own. In children, most disappear within two years. In adults, they can last much longer and often need treatment to clear faster.</span>
<h3><b>3. Can I remove my own warts?</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">You can treat some warts at home using pharmacy treatments like salicylic acid or freezing sprays. These methods take time and should not be used on the face, genitals, or if you have diabetes.</span>
<h3><b>4. What does toothpaste do to a wart?</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">There is no strong medical proof that toothpaste removes warts. It may dry the skin, but it does not kill the virus. Using toothpaste can also irritate the skin and is not recommended.</span>
