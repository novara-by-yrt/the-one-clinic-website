<h2>What Is a Cyst?</h2>
A cyst is a closed pocket of tissue. It can form anywhere on the body. It can contain fluid, air, or semi-solid material.

Think of it like a small balloon under the skin. It feels like a pea or a grape. It usually grows slowly. It is usually not painful unless it gets infected.

The most important thing to know is this: Most cysts are not cancer. They are benign. However, you should not ignore them. They can grow large. They can become uncomfortable. They can look unsightly.

If you have a lump, you need to know its type. This helps you decide what to do next.
<h2>Common Types of Cysts</h2>
There are many types of cysts. Some are on the surface. Some are deeper. Here are the four most common types we treat.
<h3>Epidermoid Cysts</h3>
This is the most common type of cyst. You might find one on your face, neck, or chest. You might also see them on your back.

<strong>What they look like:</strong> They look like small bumps. The skin over them is the same colour as your normal skin. Sometimes, they look yellow. You might see a tiny black dot in the centre. We call this a punctum.

<strong>Why they happen:</strong> Your skin has layers. The top layer is the epidermis. Sometimes, cells from the top layer move deeper into the skin. They should fall off, but they do not. They multiply, form a sac, and produce keratin. Keratin is a thick, cheese-like substance. This fills the sac.

If the cyst bursts, the keratin leaks out. It smells bad. It causes redness and swelling.
<h3>Pilar Cysts</h3>
These cysts form around hair follicles. You usually find them on the scalp.

<strong>What they look like:</strong> They feel firm. They are smooth. They are often the colour of your skin. Because they are on the head, your hair might hide them. But you can feel them when you brush your hair or wash your head.

<strong>How they differ from epidermoid cysts:</strong> The sac of a pilar cyst is thicker. This makes them easier to remove in one piece. They often run in families. If your parents had them, you might get them too.

They are usually harmless. However, they can grow quite large. This can cause pressure or discomfort.
<h3>Sebaceous Cysts</h3>
People often use this name for all skin cysts. This is incorrect. True sebaceous cysts are rare.

<strong>Where they form:</strong> They come from sebaceous glands. These glands make oil for your hair and skin. If the gland blocks, oil builds up. A cyst forms.

<strong>What to look for:</strong> You find them on the face, neck, or torso. They contain sebum (oil), not keratin. They are less common than epidermoid cysts.
<h3>Ganglion Cysts</h3>
These are different from the skin cysts above. They form near joints. You usually see them on the wrist or hand. You might also get them on the ankle or foot.

<strong>What they feel like:</strong> They feel like a round lump. They can be soft or very firm. They contain a jelly-like fluid.

<strong>Why they matter:</strong> They can press on nerves. This causes pain. It can make your hand feel weak. Sometimes, they disappear on their own. Other times, they stay and grow. If they cause pain, treat them.

<h2>Causes of Cysts and When to Seek Help</h2>
You might wonder, 'Why did I get this?'

Usually, there is no obvious reason. It is not because you are dirty. It is not because of your diet.

Common causes include:
<ul>
 	<li><strong>Blocked ducts:</strong> A hair follicle or oil gland gets blocked. Fluid builds up.</li>
 	<li><strong>Cell damage:</strong> An injury to the skin can push cells inward.</li>
 	<li><strong>Genetics:</strong> Some people are simply more prone to them.</li>
</ul>
<h3>When should you see a doctor?</h3>
Most cysts are safe. But you should seek advice if you notice changes.

Watch for these signs:
<ul>
 	<li><strong>Pain:</strong> The lump hurts when you touch it.</li>
 	<li><strong>Redness:</strong> The skin around the lump looks red or hot.</li>
 	<li><strong>Size:</strong> The lump grows fast.</li>
 	<li><strong>Location:</strong> The lump is in a place that bothers you.</li>
</ul>
If a cyst bursts, it can cause an infection. The area becomes swollen. It becomes tender. If this happens, you need medical help straightaway. Do not wait.

Similar to how you would not ignore a changing <a href="/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-warts-safely">wart on your skin</a>, a cyst that changes in size, colour, or texture also deserves prompt attention from a professional.
<h2>Diagnosis and Treatment Options</h2>
How do we know it is a cyst? How do we fix it?
<h3>How We Diagnose It</h3>
At The One Clinic, we look at the lump. We feel it. Usually, this is enough. We can distinguish a cyst from other conditions.

Sometimes, we might suggest an ultrasound. This lets us see inside the lump. It helps us check if it is solid or filled with fluid.
<h3>How We Treat It</h3>
You have a few options.
<h3>1. Watch and Wait</h3>
If the cyst is small, you can leave it. It might not bother you. However, it will not disappear on its own. It might grow larger over time.
<h3>2. Drainage</h3>
A doctor can cut the cyst and drain the fluid. This shrinks the lump.

The problem: The sac stays behind. The cyst often fills up again. We do not recommend this as a permanent fix.
<h3>3. Surgical Removal (Excision)</h3>
This is the best option. It is the gold standard.

We numb the area with a local anaesthetic. You are awake, but you feel no pain. We make a small cut. We remove the entire cyst, including the sac. Then, we close the skin with stitches.

<strong>Why this is better:</strong> Because we remove the sac, the cyst cannot come back. We send the tissue to a lab for analysis. This confirms it is benign. You get peace of mind.

<h2>FAQs</h2>
<h3><strong>1. Will the cyst go away on its own?</strong></h3>
No. A cyst might stay the same size. It often grows larger over time. The sac remains under the skin unless removed. It does not simply disappear.
<h3><strong>2. Can I squeeze or pop the lump?</strong></h3>
Please do not do this. Squeezing pushes the infection deeper. It damages your skin. It increases the risk of a scar. You cannot remove the sac yourself.
<h3><strong>3. Does cyst removal hurt?</strong></h3>
No. We use a local anaesthetic. You are awake during the procedure. However, you do not feel pain. We make sure you are comfortable.
<h3><strong>4. Why is surgery better than drainage?</strong></h3>
Drainage only removes the fluid. The sac stays behind. The cyst usually fills up again. Surgical excision removes the sac. This stops the cyst from returning.
<h3><strong>5. Is the lump cancerous?</strong></h3>
Most cysts are benign. This means they are not cancer. We send the tissue to a lab for analysis to confirm this. It gives you peace of mind.
